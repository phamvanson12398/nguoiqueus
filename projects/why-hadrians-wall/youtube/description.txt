In 122 AD the most powerful empire on Earth stopped conquering and built a 73-mile wall across Britain. So why did Hadrian's Wall have a gate every single mile?

We start on a freezing, foggy night on the wall with a nineteen-year-old soldier, a mysterious stranger, and a goat. Then we go back to meet Hadrian, the bearded emperor who decided Rome had grown big enough, and to see why Britain was the messiest border in the empire.

You'll watch three legions build eighty Roman miles of stone and turf, see the plan change halfway through, and read the real letters soldiers left behind at Vindolanda: birthday invitations, socks, underpants, and a desperate request for more beer. Finally we test the obvious theory that the wall was a fortress built to stop invasions, and find out what it was really for.
