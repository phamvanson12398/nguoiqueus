Why were Roman roads so straight, straight enough to march right into a swamp? The obvious answer is speed. The real answer starts with a wooden stick and smoke on the hilltops.

In 312 BC, at war with the Samnites, Rome began the Via Appia, and the straight Roman road was born. But going straight was expensive, and on steep hills Roman roads didn't stay straight at all. So what was really going on?

Follow a young surveyor named Marcus, a furious centurion and a doubtful local guide named Bran on the edge of Roman Britain. Along the way you'll see how the groma worked, how surveyors sighted from hill to hill, and why some of those 2,000-year-old lines still sit under modern roads like the A5.
